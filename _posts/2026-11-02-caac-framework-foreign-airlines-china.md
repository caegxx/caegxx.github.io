An airline receives an airport slot confirmation and describes the China route as approved. Another team receives an operations document and assumes the new destination can go on sale. Both statements can hide unfinished work.

Mainland China's framework involves several authorities and approval objects. The planner's first task is to identify what each document actually authorises, then keep the route, aircraft, dates, and schedule consistent across them.

This article concerns foreign airlines operating to mainland China. Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan involve distinct arrangements and are not treated here as interchangeable mainland entry cases. The framework below reflects official material checked in November 2026, including the revised Civil Aviation Law effective from 1 July 2026.

## 1. Start with the current legal foundation

The revised [Civil Aviation Law, Chapter XI](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/FLFG/202512/t20251227_229597.html) is an essential starting point. Article 204 expressly connects scheduled foreign-airline operations with home-government designation, route operating permission, and operational permission. The same chapter addresses security-programme filing, approved schedules or flight plans, Chinese-language conditions and complaint handling, and the prohibition on carrying traffic between two mainland points.

That legal architecture does not collapse into a single generic “China permit.” It requires the carrier to establish both market access and operational authority, alongside the applicable flight arrangements.

The practical consequence is straightforward: record the object and scope of every approval. A reference number without its approved route, operation type, aircraft scope, and effective conditions is an incomplete planning record.

## 2. Use five separate status lines

| Workstream | The question it answers | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic rights and designation | Is the proposed operation supported by the applicable intergovernmental arrangements and designation? | Relevant rights basis and designation evidence |
| Route operating permission | May this foreign carrier commercially operate the specified route? | CAAC route permit and its conditions |
| CCAR-129 operational scope | Is the carrier authorised for the intended operation, aircraft, and relevant operating scope? | Issued and accepted Operations Specifications |
| Airport slots | Is the planned use of coordinated airport capacity confirmed? | Applicable slot confirmation and changes |
| Schedules and flight plans | Are the intended operating dates and flight arrangements approved through the applicable process? | Seasonal approval, pre-flight plan approval, and amendments as applicable |

This table is a project-management map, not a claim that every case has exactly five administrative acts. Special operations, dangerous goods, security, or other circumstances can create additional requirements.

A useful launch report says “slots confirmed; route permission pending; operational amendment under review.” It is more informative than a single green or amber label for “China approval.”

## 3. Market access: designation is not the route permit

The [Provisions on Route Operating Permits for Foreign Air Carriers, CCAR-287](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/MHGZ/201605/t20160530_37662.html), establish the route-permit application framework. Articles 2 and 3 address the relevant intergovernmental basis and designation; Article 5 specifies submission at least 60 days before the planned start, subject to the stated agreement exception.

The application identifies the proposed route and operational particulars. Do not interpret the filing lead time as a promise that every route will be cleared within 60 days. Incomplete materials, unresolved rights, and matters outside that application can still control the launch date.

For a fifth-freedom proposal, analyse the traffic being carried on the intermediate or beyond sector separately. An airport's willingness to accommodate the flight does not resolve the intergovernmental traffic entitlement.

Maintain a route-rights note that identifies the operator, route, permitted points, traffic freedoms, frequency conditions, and unresolved interpretation. Quote the actual instrument internally where needed. A generic statement that an agreement is “open skies” is too imprecise to support a launch decision.

## 4. CCAR-129: prepare early, respect the formal dependency

CAAC's [AC-129-FS-001R2](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/GFXWJ/202406/t20240620_224536.html), issued in June 2024, describes the management of foreign carriers' CCAR-129 Operations Specifications. An [English reference translation](https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/GFXWJ/202406/P020240809572699045292.pdf) is available; the Chinese source remains important for interpretation.

Section 6 places the initial scheduled-service application after obtaining CAAC's route operating permit. It also provides for consultation with the responsible regional administration and formal application through the FSOP system. Sections 6 and 7 distinguish initial issuance from amendments.

The planning lesson is to separate preparation from formal acceptance. Manuals, aircraft records, contacts, and consistency checks can be prepared early. That does not remove the documented dependency for the initial application.

For an existing operator, inspect the actual approved scope before adding an airport, aircraft, or operational capability. The fact that one aircraft type already flies to China does not establish approval for another type. The same applies to special operational authorisations and wet-lease arrangements.

Keep one controlled data sheet for the carrier name, home AOC, aircraft identifiers, airport list, and supporting document versions. Reusing inconsistent names or outdated aircraft records across applications creates avoidable review questions.

## 5. A slot is capacity evidence

An airport slot confirms an allocation of coordinated capacity within its applicable conditions. It does not itself confer traffic rights or operational permission.

China's [national service guide for flight-plan approval](https://gjzwfw.www.gov.cn/fwmh/item/v3/item_1110000000001417041000166011000.do) makes this separation visible: the published materials include route operating approval and coordinated-airport slot confirmation, while the acceptance conditions also refer to the required operating permissions and certification.

Use that guide as a process reference, while checking current instructions in the live application system. Its page still cites an earlier version of the Civil Aviation Law, so it should not replace the revised law as the legal source or be treated as proof that every displayed procedural detail has been updated.

For the schedule planner, maintain a comparison between requested, slot-confirmed, commercially approved, and flight-plan-approved schedules. Record dates, day-of-week masks, flight numbers, aircraft type, and time basis explicitly.

A common control failure is comparing an airport's local-time confirmation against an application in UTC without preserving the conversion. Another is carrying an old operating period into a new season. A structured comparison catches both before publication.

## 6. Seasonal programmes and changes remain controlled

CCAR-287 addresses seasonal flight programmes, changes, additional services, and traffic statistics. Its renewal provisions specify an application before permit expiry; Article 28 requires the previous month's traffic statistics before the 15th of each month. Exact filing scope and current submission instructions should be checked with the responsible authority.

Do not assume that a commercially sensible adjustment is automatically covered by an earlier approval. A frequency increase, new routing, equipment change, or cancellation can affect different records. Identify which approvals need amendment and which notifications are required.

For example, replacing one aircraft type with another may require an operational-scope check even when the flight number and times remain unchanged. Adding a new intermediate point can affect the traffic-rights analysis as well as the schedule. Changing only a time can still require slot and flight-plan coordination.

Maintain an amendment log that links the commercial decision to each affected approval, submission, response, and effective date. Keep superseded versions available for audit, but prevent them from being mistaken for the current authority.

## 7. Adjacent obligations need their own owners

The launch team should assign responsibility for security, station readiness, passenger documentation, and customer-facing compliance. Include the revised law's Chinese-language conditions and complaint-handling requirements in the legal and customer-service review, rather than leaving them to the airport station at the last moment.

Local representation is another separate subject. The Ministry of Transport's [2018 rules on foreign-airline resident representative offices](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/jigou/fgs/202006/t20200623_3307962.html) establish their own approval framework. A GSA contract should not be assumed to establish a resident representative office or to satisfy every regulatory contact requirement.

State the precise role being procured from an agent: application support, regulatory liaison, sales representation, station services, or another defined task. Ask which obligations remain with the airline and retain copies of official submissions and responses.

Similarly, distinguish government charges from an agent's professional fees and airport or handling expenses. A quote for “permit support” should identify the approval, deliverable, payment trigger, and treatment of changes or cancellation.

## 8. Build the launch decision around evidence

For each workstream, track five fields: owner, submitted scope, authority reference, effective conditions, and next dependency. Add a sixth field for unresolved questions requiring an authoritative answer.

A hypothetical route might have confirmed slots and completed manuals, but still be awaiting market-access approval. Its status is preparation complete for some workstreams, with an unresolved critical dependency. Calling the whole project 80% approved can obscure the fact that the remaining item determines whether the flight can operate at all.

Run a final consistency review before the first flight. Confirm that the carrier, route, dates, aircraft, and schedule match across the current approval set. Repeat the relevant checks after any late change. The review should establish operational readiness from the actual documents, rather than from a slide showing that applications were submitted.

## Closing

China entry becomes easier to manage when the team distinguishes market access, operational scope, airport capacity, and flight arrangements. Each answers a different question, and the final operation must satisfy them together.

The planner's contribution is to make those dependencies visible early and keep the approved operation consistent as the commercial plan changes.
