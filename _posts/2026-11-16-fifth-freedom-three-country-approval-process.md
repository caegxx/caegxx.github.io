A fifth-freedom route can have a confirmed airport slot, a supportive home authority, and an enthusiastic local agent while one essential traffic entitlement remains unresolved. The individual workstreams appear to be progressing, but they do not yet describe an operation the airline can perform.

The challenge is to connect the commercial proposal with the authority required for each traffic flow, then carry the same operation through the relevant national processes. That calls for more precision than a single status line labelled “fifth freedom pending”.

This article uses a fictional airline based in State A, proposing A–B–C and the reverse routing through two foreign States. “Three-country process” is a planning framework, rather than a claim that every jurisdiction issues the same three permits. The applicable agreements and national rules determine the actual steps.

## 1 Describe the traffic before seeking the permission

Start with the complete operation: carrier, home State, routing, airports, frequency, aircraft, proposed dates, and the passengers or cargo to be carried between each pair of points. Include the return routing and any planned change of aircraft or operating carrier.

For the simple example, distinguish three commercial markets:

| **Traffic sold by the State A carrier** | **Proposed carriage** | **Planning issue** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A–B and B–A | Between the home State and State B | Applicable third- and fourth-freedom entitlements and carrier permissions |
| A–C and C–A through B | Between the home State and State C via an intermediate point | Applicable home-to-destination rights and permitted routing through B |
| B–C and C–B | Local traffic between two foreign States on the service linked to A | Fifth-freedom entitlement and its conditions at both foreign endpoints |

An aircraft movement and a passenger market are different objects. The fact that the aircraft flies B–C does not establish permission to sell and carry passengers whose journey begins in B and ends in C.

The Civil Aviation Authority of Macao's explanation of the freedoms provides a useful reference for the traffic distinction. The fifth freedom concerns carriage between the granting territory and a third State by a foreign carrier; the seventh does not require the service to connect with the carrier's home State. \[1\] If the proposed structure changes, reassess its classification instead of relying on the label originally used for the project.

## 2 Read each route schedule from the relevant State's perspective

The same point can have different roles in different agreements. In an A–C agreement, B may be an intermediate point. In an A–B agreement, C may be a beyond point. Approval of one relationship should not be assumed to answer the other.

Read the main agreement, route schedule, protocols, amendments, and relevant agreed records together. Identify whether points are already named, may be selected under an existing mechanism, or require further agreement. Record any frequency, capacity, traffic, or operational conditions attached to the grant.

ICAO's WASA explanatory notes distinguish rights that require subsequent agreement from rights whose exercise only requires future specification of points. They also explain that database summaries do not replace the agreement's wording. \[2\] That distinction can determine whether the next task is evidence of an existing entitlement, selection of a point, or a new intergovernmental discussion.

A route-planning map will not answer that legal question. Keep a written interpretation beside the relevant text and refer unresolved points to the competent authorities. Avoid treating an absent prohibition, a general liberalisation statement, or an airport's support as sufficient proof of the particular traffic right.

## 3 Map consent and permissions to the responsible authority

The commercial proposal needs a basis for the intended carriage at both B and C, along with the relevant home-State actions. A commercial agreement between agents cannot substitute for that authority.

The starting legal principle is Article 6 of the Chicago Convention: scheduled international services require the relevant State's permission or authorisation and compliance with its terms. \[3\] The applicable air services instruments and national permissions establish how that principle is implemented in the actual case.

A useful project record separates the following questions:

| **Approval object** | **Question to resolve** | **Evidence the team should identify** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intergovernmental traffic basis | Does the applicable arrangement support the proposed local B–C traffic and routing? | Relevant agreed provisions and any required confirmation or further instrument |
| Home-State carrier action | Has A completed the required designation, approval, or notification? | Applicable official record and its scope |
| Commercial operating permission in B and C | Is this carrier permitted to provide the proposed service and traffic? | Required national economic or route authorisations |
| Safety and operational scope | Is the intended carrier and aircraft operation accepted? | Applicable foreign-operator approval and relevant operational scope |
| Airport and flight arrangements | Can the proposed dates and times be used? | Slots, schedules, flight permissions, and amendments as applicable |

This is a list of questions to resolve, not a universal list of certificates to apply for. Some processes combine elements; others separate them. Existing permissions may cover part of the proposal. Record that coverage rather than filing duplicate applications automatically.

An agreement between B and C ordinarily concerns its own parties' carriers and entitlements. Its existence should not be treated as proof that a State A carrier may carry the proposed traffic.

## 4 Prepare work in parallel and respect formal dependencies

The team can gather manuals, aircraft records, insurance evidence, handling arrangements, and proposed schedules while traffic-rights discussions continue. Parallel preparation shortens avoidable internal delay.

Formal applications may still have a prescribed order. China's AC-129-FS-001R2, for example, describes the initial scheduled-service CCAR-129 process after obtaining the route operating permit and provides for consultation with the responsible regional administration. \[4\] That is a concrete example of a dependency to map, rather than a sequence to impose on every foreign authority.

Likewise, Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism describes permission for foreign airlines operating international scheduled services and prior approval of relevant changes to the business plan. \[5\] The project must identify what is already authorised and what the new routing changes.

Ask each authority which materials can be prepared, which applications can be accepted now, and which decision depends on another document. Keep preparation complete, application accepted, review completed, and permission issued as distinct statuses.

A filing lead time does not establish the time needed to resolve an intergovernmental question. Put that uncertainty on the critical path separately from the administrative review of a complete carrier application.

## 5 Make the unresolved point specific enough to answer

When an authority raises an objection, record the exact issue. Is the carrier's designation missing? Is B not an agreed intermediate point? Is local B–C traffic outside the scope? Is a frequency limit exhausted? Or does the aircraft or operation require another approval?

The response should match the issue. A useful clarification request identifies the proposed routing and traffic, cites the relevant provision, explains the airline's understanding, and asks the authority to identify the remaining action and responsible channel.

For example, the team might seek confirmation of whether the existing point-selection procedure is sufficient or whether additional agreement is required. That is more actionable than asking broadly whether fifth-freedom operations are allowed.

If a further government instrument is necessary, identify who may negotiate or sign it and when it takes effect. ICAO's Template Air Services Agreements illustrate that designation, authorisation, consultation, amendment, and entry into force are separate subjects that States may structure differently. \[6\] A document called an MoU should be assessed by its content, authority, and effect, rather than its title alone.

Retain the authority's response and any conditions in the project record. An agent's summary can help the team understand progress, but a consequential interpretation should be traceable to the official record on which the airline relies.

## 6 Keep one operation consistent across three processes

Late changes can create a mismatch even after significant progress. A different intermediate airport may affect the route-rights basis. A new aircraft type may require an operational amendment. A change in days or times may affect airport capacity and the submitted programme.

Maintain one controlled schedule with a revision identifier. It should state flight numbers, routing, operation dates, days of week, aircraft, times, time zone, and day offsets. Each application should identify which revision it contains.

Compare the approved scope with that schedule, rather than merely checking that a letter has arrived. Record limitations on frequencies, traffic types, carriers, aircraft, and validity. Distinguish approval from a conditional offer or an acknowledgement of receipt.

Airport slots are a separate constraint. IATA explicitly distinguishes a slot from route rights and other operational permissions. \[7\] A confirmed slot can therefore coexist with an unresolved traffic-rights issue. Equally, a traffic entitlement can exist without usable airport capacity at the proposed time.

For a through rotation, test the entire day after any retiming. A change at B can break the turn at C, the return slot, or the crew duty pattern. The project is ready only for the operation the combined current approvals and operational plan support.

## 7 Evaluate the commercial fallback before committing to it

If local B–C traffic remains unapproved, a proposal to carry only A–C through passengers needs its own authority and business case. Removing local sales may alter the forecast sharply, and it does not automatically establish that the revised routing is permitted.

Use a simple hypothetical contribution test. Suppose the commercial plan expects 25 local B–C passengers per one-way departure, each contributing USD 80 after passenger-related expenses. Losing that traffic removes USD 2,000 from the departure's contribution before any resulting cost or network changes. The remaining plan must be recalculated rather than described as substantially unchanged.

Other alternatives might include deferral, another agreed intermediate point, reduced frequency, or a different route structure. Each changes the question being assessed. A technical stop does not confer local traffic rights, and describing a service as a charter does not make the scheduled-service assumptions or permissions transferable.

Compare the feasible alternatives with an explicit no-launch option. Include commitments already made, prospective cancellation costs, and the effect on the rest of the network. The original route can remain commercially attractive while still being unavailable within the intended launch window.

## 8 Separate permission to market from permission to operate

An airline needs a deliberate decision about when to announce the route, accept bookings, and commit non-refundable expenditure. The requirements can differ by jurisdiction and by the status of the relevant permissions. Obtain a specific answer for the proposed sales activity where the position is uncertain.

Maintain a record of what may be advertised or sold, under which conditions, and from which date. Conditional wording in an announcement should not be assumed to resolve an unresolved sales restriction or a missing operating authorisation.

Work backwards from a realistic first-flight date. Allow time after the final critical decision for distribution setup, crew and station preparation, and the remaining operational steps. If the approval moves beyond the commercially useful booking window, revisit the launch decision even when the legal position appears close to resolution.

At the final review, state the remaining dependencies individually. A route that has slots and prepared manuals but lacks an essential traffic entitlement is not meaningfully described by an average percentage complete. Management needs to know which decision still determines whether the planned service can operate.

## Closing

A fifth-freedom project becomes manageable when every proposed traffic flow has an identified authority basis and every application has a defined dependency. The route schedule, carrier permissions, operational scope, and flight arrangements must describe the same intended service.

The most useful progress report names the unresolved point, the authority that can resolve it, the evidence required, and the consequence for launch. That gives the airline a practical next action and a defensible decision about when to commit.
